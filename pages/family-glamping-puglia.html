Family glamping in Puglia: what to look for (really)

Space, practicality, something to do. No compromises.

Back to home

Taking children to a glamping place can be a wonderful experience or a logistical disaster, depending on the place. Children need room to move, accessible toilets, and something to do nearby.

Not every glamping place in Puglia suits families. Some are clearly designed for couples. Others have a more inclusive spirit, with double or family-size tents and an organisation that makes daily life less complicated.

Space comes first

The first thing to check is the size of the accommodation. Some safari tents are designed for two people, and adding a third bed makes them crowded. Places that state a capacity of "up to 4 people" with the beds already set up are generally the ones that have really thought about it.

Wooden lodges or geodesic domes tend to have more volume than classic tents, and some places offer modular units — a sleeping area for the parents separate from the children’s. This detail, which may sound like a luxury, becomes practical when you travel with small children.

Location: close to what?

For a family, the position of a glamping place matters differently than for a couple. It is not just aesthetics: it is logistics. It is important to know how far the nearest supermarket is, whether there is a beach reachable within a reasonable time, and whether the restaurants nearby suit children too.

Places in Puglia’s countryside — olive groves, masserie — can be beautiful, but they always require a car for any trip. That is not a problem in itself, but it is worth knowing in advance.

What families value

Looking at what guests say about places that also welcome families, a few constants emerge. Clean bathrooms come up very often — with small children, a well-kept and easily accessible bathroom changes the quality of the stay.

Equipped green spaces are appreciated. Breakfast included, or easy to prepare yourself, often turns out to be a comfort factor. And welcoming staff who can engage with children without being intrusive are almost always singled out in a positive way.

Wishne, near Bisceglie, is a good example of a place that works for families as well as couples: olive-grove space for children to roam, a play area, a small pool, bicycles and a friendly dog on site.

Valle d’Itria and Fasano: the more versatile area

For families, the area between Fasano, Locorotondo and Ostuni probably offers the best balance between natural setting and accessible services. There are enough attractions nearby — the Fasano safari zoo, the beaches of Torre Canne, the historic villages — to build a varied stay.

The Gargano is an interesting alternative for families who love wild nature. Transfer times between things to see are longer, however, and some places are more isolated.

Something that usually goes unsaid

The glamping places best suited to families are rarely the ones with the most aggressive marketing. They are often family-run, with owners on site who have learned over time what their guests need and adapted.

An extra tent for luggage, a shady corner for lunch, a tip on where to take the children for a swim without the crowds. It is not revolutionary, but it is exactly what you need.
