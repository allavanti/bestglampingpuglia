Family glamping in Puglia: what to look for (really)

Where children have things to do, where more care is needed, and how to choose the right tent.

Back to home

With children a glamping works or does not work for very practical reasons: room to move, a bathroom that is easy to reach, something to do without taking the car. Of the five places we have profiled, two really think this way, and a third does so in its own manner.

Where children have things to do

Le Lenze Don Mimì, at Mattinatella on the Gargano, has what a family needs: a pool, a playground (check in season), a restaurant where you eat without organising, an olive grove to run in and a pebble bay five minutes away on foot. The family that runs it is present, and it shows. Our advice is to choose a big tent: with four people the difference is felt.

Tenuta DonnAnna, at Galatone, is a farm stay with tents in the Salento: an always-available pool, home-made breakfast, dinners, cooking classes. The sea is a quarter of an hour by car, so for the children the day happens at the pool and the sea is an outing. Mosquitoes, in the evening, are part of the deal.

Big tents and a shared kitchen: Macaria

Macaria Camp, at Capilungo on the Ionian Salento, has big tents among olive trees, each with a hammock and a gazebo, and a shared kitchen: for a family that means being able to make a simple meal when needed. The sea is about 800 metres away. Shade is scarce and the facilities are shared: it is a comfortable-campsite solution, for children who are not put off by heat and a few walks.

Beautiful places, but made for grown-ups

HMO welcomes children gladly, and for them sleeping in a tent like this is an adventure; but the sea drops away quickly and the climb back from the bay in the heat is long. With small children it needs attention, and the golf cart helps. Wishne is a simple place, with a compost bathroom next to the tent: excellent for children used to the outdoors, less so for anyone expecting hotel comforts.

What to look at before booking

How many people really fit in the tent, and with which beds. Whether the bathroom is your own and how far away. Whether there is something to do without the car (a pool, a lawn) and how many minutes the sea is. How hot it gets in the middle hours, and whether there is shade. For the full picture of the five places there is the general guide.

The properties best suited to families are often family-run, and over time have understood what their guests need.
