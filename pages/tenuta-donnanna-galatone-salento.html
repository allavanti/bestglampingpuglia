Tenuta DonnAnna: family hospitality, the sea a quarter of an hour away

A farm stay with tents in the Salento countryside, at Galatone. You choose it for the table and the people, not for a beach below the door.

What it isFarm stay with rooms and glamping
TableHome-style breakfast, dinners and cooking classes
The seaSeveral beaches about 15 minutes by car

There is a kind of place you remember for the people before the tents. Tenuta DonnAnna belongs to that category: you arrive to sleep in a green park in the Salento, and you leave talking about breakfast and the person who prepares it.

Maddalena, Donna Anna and a family that opens its home

Guests are welcomed by Maddalena, with her mother, Donna Anna, and the rest of the family, each with their own part in the life of the estate. The welcome is that of people opening the doors of their home: you feel expected, you receive advice at just the right moment, and you often end up chatting for a long time. It is not a place that merely hosts: it shares its story, its cooking, its traditions.

Glamping in the orchard, and rooms with a patio

The tents, new and beautifully kept, stand in an orchard, with sofas and hammocks outside for the slow hours of the afternoon. The idea is a greener stay, with all the comforts of a room and the scent of the countryside around you.

For anyone who prefers walls, the cottages are spacious, with a private patio and direct access to the garden, really comfortable beds and everything you need, with nothing left to chance.

The table: orecchiette, jams and a shared table

Breakfast is sweet and savoury, with local, home-made produce: jams, fruit from the organic garden, fresh pasta. You can join a cooking class, learning orecchiette and then sitting down to lunch with the family and the other guests at a shared table. Some evenings there are also small, limited-seating dinners, with a welcome toast and the story of the estate.

Pool, garden and four-legged friends

The pool is large, with gazebos and sunbeds set well apart, so everyone finds their own corner of peace, and it can be used at any hour. Around it is a beautifully kept garden, and across the lawns dogs and cats are ready for a cuddle, beginning with Bilù, the family dog.

What to keep in mind

We are inland, and it is best to know that before picturing a stroll to the beach. The sea is about a quarter of an hour away by car, and you need a car to get around, which for many people is actually a plus, because the calm stays intact. In summer mosquitoes and other insects make themselves felt, so repellent is a must. Dinners are not guaranteed every evening: ask in advance, and think of them as something special rather than a daily service.

There is free parking and Wi-Fi, a pool, a bar, excursions and complimentary toiletries. Restaurants and attractions are within a kilometre. The nearest airport is Brindisi, about 59 km away.

Nearby

Galatone is best explored on foot. The highlight is the Sanctuary of the Santissimo Crocifisso della Pietà, built between 1683 and 1694, with a baroque facade in carparo and Lecce stone. Then the Collegiata dell’Assunta, the Porta di San Sebastiano (the only one left of the Aragonese walls) and the Palazzo Marchesale.

For the sea, distances range from 10 to 15 km depending on where you set off. The best-known beaches are Santa Maria al Bagno, small and with calm, shallow water, and La Reggia, in the same resort, where the seabed drops away quickly and the water is good for snorkelling but not ideal for small children. With children, choose beaches with a gradual entry. Gallipoli is about 13 km away and Lecce about 24; from here you can reach both the Ionian and the Adriatic coast in 15–40 minutes, and even Otranto is within reach for a day trip.

Who we recommend it to

Anyone seeking a slower Salento, made of pool, table and people, who doesn’t mind driving a quarter of an hour to the sea. Couples, friends and families who love a countryside setting, well kept and homely, and anyone travelling with a dog. Less so for anyone who wants to wake to the sound of waves.

In the end what stays with you is breakfast, the pasta rolled out together, the dog keeping you company in the garden and Donna Anna telling how the estate began: the Salento you take home with you.
