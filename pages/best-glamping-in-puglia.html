The best glamping in Puglia: where to really sleep outdoors

Five places profiled up close, from the Gargano to the Salento, and how to choose the right one.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Back to home

Under the label “glamping” in Puglia you will find almost anything: tents furnished like hotel rooms, campsites with a few extra cushions, farm stays that put two tents in the olive grove. To see what to expect we have profiled five places up close, from the Gargano to the Salento. This guide lines them up the way people actually choose: what you are after, how far you are willing to walk, how much comfort you need.

What we call glamping

A real tent, a real bed and a bathroom of your own or at least a private one, in a place with a reason to be there: an olive grove, a bay, a masseria. After that things vary a lot. At Bisceglie the tent is simple and sits in a field; at Vignanotica it is more like a suite with canvas walls, so much so that guests hesitate to call it a tent; at Galatone it is one more room of a farm stay. None of the three is wrong: they are three different promises, and trouble starts when you expect one and book another.

In the places that work there is almost always a family, or two hosts, who live there. You can feel it.

Three landscapes, five addresses

North of Bari, among olive trees. Wishne, near Bisceglie, is an olive field in the Lama Santa Croce park, a few minutes from Trani: tents, hammocks, a trullo or two, dinners with the hosts. It is the simplest and most homely of the five, and for that reason it explains itself. Keep in mind: the bathroom sits next to the tent, private but of the compost type.

On the Gargano, with the sea below. Le Lenze Don Mimì, at Mattinatella, is an olive grove looking over a pebble bay, with a pool, a restaurant, a hot tub on the veranda and a flight of steps down to the sea in a few minutes. Further south, at Vignanotica, HMO is the more refined version: tents with a private garden and a tub, a bay five minutes away on foot and no mobile signal. In both cases you need a car, and in summer you feel the heat.

In the Salento, two ways of staying. Tenuta DonnAnna, at Galatone, is a farm stay with tents: home-made breakfast, a pool, dinners, cooking classes, a family welcome; the sea is a quarter of an hour by car, and mosquitoes are part of the deal. Macaria Camp, at Capilungo on the Ionian side, has big tents among olive trees with a hammock and a gazebo, about 800 metres from the sea; the kitchen is shared, the facilities are shared and there is little shade.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

How to choose

If the sea is the reason for the trip, the Gargano: Le Lenze or HMO, with the bay on foot. Those who want the coast with more freedom to move look at the Ionian Salento, and Macaria. In our piece on seaside glamping we explain how to tell real access from a mere view.

If you want quiet for two, HMO for suite comfort and a private tub; Wishne for a simple place among the olive trees and the company of the hosts. The page on romantic glamping compares them closely.

If you travel with children, space matters, and so does having something to do without taking the car: a pool, a lawn, a playground. Le Lenze and DonnAnna think this way. The rest is in the guide to family glamping.

If you want the fewest surprises, a property with a restaurant and real services (Le Lenze, HMO) takes every worry away; one with a shared kitchen like Macaria is freer and less comfortable.

What to ask before booking

Four questions avoid almost every surprise. Is the bathroom inside, next to the tent or shared? How far is the sea, and on foot or by car? Is there shade, and how is it at night in the heat? What is for dinner, and does it have to be booked? The answers are in our profiles, but a direct message to the property always clears up the details that change from year to year.

When to go

July and August are the hottest months, and canvas does not insulate like a wall: big tents, shade and cool nights matter more than the view. June and September give long light, warm sea and fewer people. At the end of the season some places, like HMO, still work with the same care even with very few guests.

How to book

Almost all the properties are on Booking or Airbnb and have a site of their own. Writing directly lets you ask what matters and sometimes get better terms. If the information online is thin or dated, a phone call is better.
