Seaside glamping in Puglia: read this before you book

From a bay five minutes on foot to a sea that needs the car: what to expect from each place.

Photo: Wikimedia Commons

Back to home

“Sea view” does not mean access to the sea. In Puglia the gap between the two can be a flight of steps five minutes long or a quarter of an hour by car along a provincial road, and on closeness to the sea the disappointment is the most common one. Here we put the places we have profiled in order, from the closest to the water to the furthest, with what you need to know about each.

The sea on foot, on the Gargano

The Gargano is where coastal glamping makes the most sense: high coast, woods, bays reachable only on foot, a landscape found nowhere else. Two of our places are here.

Le Lenze Don Mimì, at Mattinatella, sits in an olive grove looking over a pebble bay: a flight of steps takes you to the beach in about five minutes. It is not sand, and that is worth knowing: you go with the right shoes, and the way back, in the hot hours, is felt. In exchange the pool is right there, and the bay is small and quiet.

HMO, at Vignanotica, is about 450 metres from the bay, beneath the white cliff. You walk down in five minutes or take the electric golf carts, which matter above all for the climb back. The beach is pebbles and the seabed drops away quickly; in summer, at weekends, boats and day visitors arrive. There are padded sunbeds for guests and canoes to see the caves.

The difference between a sea view and the sea on foot is measured in steps, not metres.

The Ionian Salento: a stroll, not a hike

Photo: Hydruntum, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

Macaria Camp, at Capilungo in the municipality of Alliste, is about 800 metres from the Ionian sea: a stroll, not a transfer. The tents are big, among olive trees, each with a hammock and a gazebo; shade is scarce, though, and the facilities are shared. It is the place for anyone who wants the sea by day and a comfortable campsite life in the evening, with the shared kitchen.

By car: lovely places, but the sea is an outing

Tenuta DonnAnna, at Galatone, and Wishne, near Bisceglie, are not seaside glamping and do not claim to be. DonnAnna has its pool and the sea about a quarter of an hour by car; Wishne is an olive grove in the Lama Santa Croce park, from which you drive to the coast. Choose them for the welcome and the place, not for the beach.

What to check before booking

Four questions, to check on a map and with the property. Is the distance to the sea as the crow flies or by the real route? Is there direct access or do you need the car? Is the beach sand, pebbles or rock? And how crowded is it in high season? A pebble bay with weekend boats is a different experience from an empty beach in June.

A closing thought

The best seaside glamping is not always the one closest to the water. Sometimes it is the one that gives you the choice: the bay five minutes away when you want to swim, the quiet of the olive grove when you want to see no one. For the full picture of the places, start from the general guide.
